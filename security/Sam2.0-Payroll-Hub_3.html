Note for build — debt repayment on Weekly Payroll: unlike special payrolls (Residual, InHouse), Weekly is where the ~50% "leave the rep something" guideline applies — but it's an unwritten policy, not a system rule. The payroll team decides case by case, weighing that week's net pay against how much debt is backlogged, and can go above or below 50% depending on the situation. Worked example of this decision lives in Debt / Collections. Not modeled as an enforced cap here — this is a policy note, not a hard constraint the UI should bake in.

Guarantee Payroll

The held commission on an account — released to the rep after 12 months + free months have passed, provided the account is in good standing at that moment. It's a snapshot check at the Out-of-Guarantee date, not a full-history one — an account that went past due mid-window but caught back up still qualifies. Visible here regardless of which office/rep you're browsing above.

Eligibility timeline: an account doesn't start earning Residual the month it's Funded. Three conditions all have to be true: (1) the account is Funded, (2) at least 3 months + however many free months were given during the sale have elapsed since Date Install — an account with 6 free months waits 9 months total before its first possible Residual payment, one with 0 free months waits 3, and (3) the customer is current on last month's payment. Miss any one of the three and that month pays $0, but only the payment-status condition resets month to month — the 3-months-plus-free-months clock is a one-time wait per account, not something that restarts.
Skipped ≠ disqualified permanently: P. Whitcomb and H. Bright didn't make their previous month's payment, so they're excluded from this month's residual — but that's a month-by-month check, not a one-time gate. They're back in as soon as payment is current again. Residual payments themselves are never charged back once paid, even if the source account later goes bad — that risk lives entirely in Weekly Payroll's Front End, not here.
Note for build — debt repayment on special payrolls: Debt can be pulled from any positive payroll, not just Weekly — the goal is recouping it as fast as possible since open debt is a liability on the company's books. But the ~50% "keep half the check" guideline that applies to Weekly Payroll is informal (payroll's judgment call, not a hard rule) and does not apply here. Residual, InHouse, and other special payrolls have no floor — the payroll team can apply debt repayment up to the full amount, and a rep's special-payroll check can land at $0.00 if debt requires it. Not yet reflected in the numbers above — this is a policy note for whoever builds the debt-application step, not a modeled example.

In House Details

Monthly, like Residual — compensation for in-house-sourced sales, separate from field/direct sales
Eligibility timeline: an In House account has two independent conditions, both required before it earns anything. (1) Free-months delay — same concept as Residual's timing, but no separate 3-month base period is specified for In House: the account waits out however many free months were given during the sale (0 free months = no delay at all) before it's even in the running. (2) Equipment + tech balance fully repaid via Weekly Payroll, independent of the free-months clock — an account past its free-months delay can still be Ineligible if that balance isn't clear yet, and vice versa. Both have to be true simultaneously for a given month to pay out.
In House accounts are paid entirely outside the normal Direct Sales Agreement — no Weekly Front End waterfall applies. Net RMR = Monthly Revenue − Pass-Through Fee − In House Fee ($15/mo for EFT or Credit Card, $16/mo for Invoice). Equipment cost and technician install cost are repaid through a one-time Weekly Payroll deduction — a completely separate system from this monthly calculation. Until both balances hit $0 there, the account is Ineligible and the company keeps 100% of that month's Net RMR: no partial payout, no credit applied toward the balance from here, and no catch-up once the account does become eligible.

Worked example — this week's payroll for A. Reyes: Net pay before debt is applied comes to $1,800.00. Payroll caps this week's debt repayment at roughly 50%, leaving $900.00 of headroom. The oldest open item ($1,157.92) is too large to take without breaking that floor, so payroll checks the next item ($965.10) — still too large — then the last one ($996.98) — also too large. Because a chargeback can't currently be split, none of A. Reyes's debt gets touched this week, even though $900.00 of headroom exists. If partial payments were supported, that $900.00 could go against the oldest item today, leaving $257.92 remaining instead of the full $1,157.92 — this is the backlog problem you described.
